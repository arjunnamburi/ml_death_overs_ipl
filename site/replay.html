Match Replay

Four 2026 matches, from over 16 to the end of each innings. For every ball: the situation, what three models predicted, what happened, and why XGBoost thought what it did.

How to read this page
  1. Pick a match and an innings. The close chase (a tie) opens first, on the first death ball. Balls are written the cricket way: 15.2 means the 2nd ball of the 16th over.
  2. The chart shows each model's running total of log-loss minus the scorecard baseline's. Below zero means the model has done better than B1 so far. Lower is better. Hover to see each ball; click to open it.
  3. The ball strip has one square per delivery: the outcome (• = dot, W = wicket), with wides and no-balls faded. A small dot in the corner marks the five balls where XGBoost and B1 differed most, and “FH” marks a free hit. Use the arrow keys or the buttons to step through.
  4. Each ball shows the scorecard before the ball (what the models saw), one sentence on whether XGBoost or B1 did better, the chance each model gave each outcome (the real outcome is outlined) and the log-loss (lower is better).
  5. The push bars are the three features that moved XGBoost’s score for what happened most, up (right) or down (left), from its starting score for that outcome. A push changes a score, not a chance: XGBoost turns the seven outcomes’ scores into chances together (softmax), so the chance also depends on the other six outcomes. The chance each model gave is in the ball’s table of chances. Hover a bar for the exact push.
  6. The sentence names the features that moved XGBoost most in the direction that decided the ball. They explain XGBoost’s own number, not B1’s, and they are the model’s reasons, not causes on the field.

How these four matches were picked

Four 2026 matches, picked by a rule written on Nov 4. By then some whole-match results on all balls had been seen (the most and least predictable matches, and one match suggested during the error analysis), but no per-match death result had been looked at. Eligible: matches where both innings reached over 16. Gain = XGBoost's mean death-ball log-loss minus B1's, over both innings. Picked in this order, each from the matches not yet picked: XGBoost's best (most negative gain); XGBoost's worst (most positive gain); typical (gain closest to the median of all eligible matches); close chase (a chase that reached the 20th over, with the smallest gap between the two innings totals; ties go to the earlier match).

Lucknow Super Giants were 92/4 after 15 overs, chasing 156. Over these 30 legal balls, XGBoost finished behind B1 (+0.91 in total log-loss) and the logistic regression finished behind B1 (+1.59).

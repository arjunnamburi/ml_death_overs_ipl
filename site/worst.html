Worst-Predicted Balls

The 2026 death balls XGBoost got most wrong, each with a hypothesis for why. Open a ball with Show why to see what pushed XGBoost away from the real outcome.

How to read this page
  • Three lists (the tabs): the ten balls with XGBoost’s highest log-loss; the ten where XGBoost lost most to the scorecard baseline (B1); and the worst three of each outcome. A ball can be in more than one list.
  • The small table gives the chance each model put on what actually happened, and the log-loss that follows from it. The lower the chance, the higher the log-loss.
  • The tag says whose miss it was. Rare for every model: even B1 gave it under 1%. XGBoost’s own miss: B1 gave it at least twice XGBoost’s chance. Shared miss: everything else.
  • The hypothesis is one of four patterns, explained below the lists. Show why gives the full reasoning for that ball.
  • The push bars (inside Show why) show which features moved XGBoost toward what happened (right) or away from it (left), compared with its guess for an average ball. As a rough guide, −0.3 cuts the chance by about a quarter, −0.7 about halves it and −1.4 cuts it to about a quarter. Hover a bar for the exact figure.
  • Pushes are the model’s reasons, not causes on the field.

Four Hypotheses, Shared Across the Balls

Each hypothesis was written from a handful of balls, then checked on all 3,583 death balls of 2026 (notebook). Three of the four don’t show up as a pattern across the season, so the balls behind them are more likely rare outcomes than blind spots in the model. The fourth, wickets by chance, matches the season.

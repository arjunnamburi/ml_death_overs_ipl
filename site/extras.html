More detail on 2026: which players and matches the model read best and worst, the full ablation tables, and every feature the models use. Like the other analyses after the test, they describe 2026 rather than test it, and nothing in the models was changed because of them.

How to read this page
  • Gain vs B1 is XGBoost’s log-loss minus the scorecard baseline’s, per ball, on that player’s or match’s balls. Negative means XGBoost read them better. The brackets are 95% intervals from resampling matches.
  • “vs expected” compares what happened with what XGBoost expected on the same balls: runs, strike rate (runs per 100 balls), sixes and wickets. Positive means more than expected.
  • Ablation tables show how much worse XGBoost got when a group of features was removed and the model refit. Positive means the group was helping.

Which Players the Model Reads Best and Worst (2026)

44 batters (at least 150 balls faced) and 61 bowlers (at least 120 balls bowled) in 2026, all overs. XGBoost reads 24 of the batters and 32 of the bowlers clearly better than B1, and no player clearly worse.

  • The best-read batters are big hitters, such as Suryavanshi, Allen and Abhishek Sharma. Their history most likely tells XGBoost they hit sixes, which B1 can’t know.
  • The worst-read batters had slower seasons than their history suggested, such as Jaiswal and Washington Sundar.
  • For bowlers it runs the other way: the worst-read conceded far fewer runs than expected, such as Ngidi and Bhuvneshwar Kumar. The model barely tells bowlers apart.
  • No player, batter or bowler, is read clearly worse than by B1.
